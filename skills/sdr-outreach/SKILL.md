# SDR Outreach Skill

## When to Use This Skill

- Running the Agentic SDR pipeline (scrape job boards → research → find contacts → draft)
- Drafting cold emails to CEOs of companies posting AI job listings
- Pitching the Free $10k AI Audit or Shiloh AI OS
- Any outbound outreach where the prospect is actively hiring for AI roles

## The Offer: Free $10k AI Audit

Lead with this offer when reaching out to companies hiring for AI:

> "I'll install Shiloh AI OS for a fraction of the cost of hiring. Free $10k AI Audit to show you what's possible."

**Why it works:** They're posting jobs for $120k+/year. You're offering to solve the same problem for a fraction of the cost. The audit proves value before they commit.

## The P&P Framework (Problem → Promise)

For cold outreach emails, use this structure:

1. **Problem** — Open with their specific problem. Reference the job they're posting (e.g., "You're hiring an AI Operations lead for $120k/year").
2. **Promise** — Your solution. "I install Shiloh AI OS for a fraction of that cost."
3. **Proof** — One line of credibility (optional).
4. **CTA** — Soft ask. "Worth a 15-min call?"

**Example structure:**
```
[Problem] You're hiring a Prompt Engineer — companies like yours are spending $150k+ to fill that role.
[Promise] I install Shiloh AI OS for a fraction of that. Free $10k AI Audit to show you what's possible.
[CTA] Worth a 15-min call?
```

## The "AI Sent This Email" Angle

When you get on the call, use this pitch:

> "The email that got you on this call was entirely researched, written, and sent by my AI OS. I was asleep when it sent it. Do you want me to build this for your sales team?"

**Why it works:** It's the ultimate flex. You're proving ROI by using the product to acquire them.

- [ ] Does the subject line reference something specific (their company, their job posting)?
- [ ] Is the problem clearly stated in the first sentence?
- [ ] Is the offer (Free $10k AI Audit) explicit?
- [ ] Is the CTA soft and low-friction?
- [ ] Under 150 words total?
