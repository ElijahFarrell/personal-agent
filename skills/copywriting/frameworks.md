# Copywriting Frameworks
## 1. The Pyramid Principle
**Use for:** All writing. The default framework for any piece of content.
**The structure:**
1. Always start with the conclusion (the big idea, the answer, the insight)
2. Prove your conclusion with 3-4 key arguments
3. Support each argument with facts, data, stories, or examples
**Why it works:**
- The reader immediately knows if it's relevant to them
- They can follow a clear logic train to your conclusion
- Eliminates the most common writing failure: burying the point
**Common mistakes to avoid:**
- Hooks that don't immediately tell the reader what they'll receive
- Writing random ideas and trying to string them into a conclusion at the end
- Starting with context instead of the conclusion
**Example structure:**
```
[CONCLUSION] AI agents will replace most SaaS tools within 3 years.
[ARGUMENT 1] Agents can now perform tasks that required dedicated software.
  [EVIDENCE] SaaS sector lost $2T in market cap in early 2026.
[ARGUMENT 2] The cost of agents is dropping faster than SaaS pricing.
  [EVIDENCE] Average AI deal size grew 340% in one year.
[ARGUMENT 3] Businesses are already cutting SaaS subscriptions.
  [EVIDENCE] 68% of enterprises now use AI in at least one function.
```

## 2. The P&P Framework
**Use for:** Social content, newsletters, book chapters, long-form posts.
**The structure:**
- **Topic** — Choose one specific topic to write about
- **Research** — Add statistics, quotes, highlights, tweets, or anything that justifies your argument
- **Problem** — List the problems and how they will impact someone's life when left unsolved
- **Process** — List the steps to solve the problem
**Instructions:** Copy these four elements into a new document as your outline before writing. Fill each section before drafting the full piece.
---
## 3. The PASTOR Framework
**Use for:** Sales copy, offer descriptions, landing pages, high-stakes persuasion.
**The structure:**
- **P — Person/Problem** — Identify the person and their core problem
- **A — Amplify** — Amplify the pain of the problem. What happens if it goes unsolved?
- **S — Story/Solution** — Tell a story that leads to your solution
- **T — Transformation** — Show the transformation your solution creates
- **O — Offer** — Present your offer clearly
- **R — Response** — Tell them exactly what to do next
---
## 4. The Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS) Framework
**Use for:** Short-form posts, email subject lines, quick hooks.
- **Problem** — State the problem clearly
- **Agitate** — Make the reader feel the pain of the problem
- **Solve** — Present the solution
---
## 5. The Before-After-Bridge (BAB) Framework
**Use for:** Testimonials, case studies, transformation stories.
- **Before** — Describe the world before your solution
- **After** — Describe the world after your solution
- **Bridge** — Your solution is the bridge between the two
---
## 6. The AIDA Framework
**Use for:** Outreach messages, cold emails, ads.
- **Attention** — Grab attention with a bold statement or question
- **Interest** — Build interest with relevant information
- **Desire** — Create desire by showing the benefit
- **Action** — Tell them exactly what to do
---
## Framework Selection Guide
| Content Type | Best Framework |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn post | Pyramid Principle |
| Newsletter | P&P Framework |
| Sales copy | PASTOR |
| Short post/tweet | PAS |
| Case study | BAB |
| Outreach DM | AIDA |
| Any content | Pyramid Principle (default) |
